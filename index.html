How to reduce screen time on reels (5 simple steps)
  • Measure first. You can’t cut what you can’t see. Count your short videos for a few days without trying to change anything.
  • Set a realistic limit. Start about 20–30% below your normal day, not at zero.
  • Use gentle reminders. A nudge at 75% gives you a moment to choose, without the stress of a hard block.
  • Keep your number visible. A home-screen widget makes you aware before you even open the app.
  • Replace the habit. Plan something for your most common scrolling time, like after lunch or before sleep.

PAICONS Self-Development does steps 1–4 for you automatically.

What is a dopamine detox, and does it work for reels?

A “dopamine detox” usually means taking a break from quick, endless rewards such as short videos, notifications and games, so that slower activities feel rewarding again.

For most people, a sudden full stop doesn’t last. Cutting down gradually works better: know your number, choose a limit, and lower it week by week. PAICONS Self-Development is a self-awareness tool that helps you do this. It isn’t medical treatment.

Digital wellbeing app vs app blocker: which is better?

App blockers lock apps when you hit a limit, and many people end up switching them off. A digital wellbeing approach builds awareness first: it shows your real numbers and nudges you, so stopping becomes your own decision.

PAICONS Self-Development never blocks your apps. It helps you notice, then choose.

Signs your phone or social media use is too much
  • You open reels “for a minute” and lose much longer.
  • You scroll late at night and sleep less.
  • You reach for your phone automatically when bored or stressed.
  • You feel worse, not better, after scrolling.

If you notice these, start by measuring. If phone use is seriously affecting your health or daily life, please talk to a qualified professional.
